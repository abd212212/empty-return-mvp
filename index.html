      Prototype for the 2026 Sustainability Hackathon, built for the IKEA challenge. Offer rules: we pay IKEA's published Buyback rates of 45 / 35 / 25 % of the original price for grades A / B / C, plus the standard 15 % IKEA Family uplift. Unlike Buyback we collect, and unlike Buyback we want the piece taken apart. Minimum original price of NT$3,700 / 4,500 / 8,700 by grade, set so that every piece at the minimum still pays even when collected alone, with every bonus and the IKEA Family uplift included. Pieces collected alongside a paid one carry no credit and need only have cost about NT$1,000 new, because they skip refurbishment and go to the Circular Hub as they are. Bonuses are flat amounts, because the savings they create are measured in crew minutes and load metres, not in the value of the piece. Upholstery, mattresses, rugs, electricals and lighting are out of phase one. Product list and original prices: current prices on ikea.com.tw, accessed 7 November 2026, used as a stand-in for the price the customer paid.

      Impact is reported at 3.62 kg CO₂e per kg: 3.9 kg avoided less 0.28 kg to run the programme, both from published research on a municipal reuse scheme. That research also finds rebound effects of 82 to 167 % for furniture, driven by how people re-spend the money. We pay in store credit rather than cash precisely because it makes that re-spending visible and steerable instead of unknown. The figure shown is therefore avoided emissions net of operations, not a rebound-adjusted net effect, and we do not claim it as one.

      Same cost model as our proposal and tour simulator. Stated assumptions, none from IKEA: two-person crew NT$800 per hour, 8 minutes set-up per stop shared by all pieces there, 6 minutes per disassembled and 18 per assembled piece, NT$12 per vehicle km, NT$120 hub handling and grading per paid piece. Resale at 50 / 40 / 25 percent of original price for grades A / B / C, as in our break-even calculation, refurbishment NT$350, credit at IKEA Buyback rates costing (1 - 8 % unredeemed) x (1 - gross margin) of face value. Free extra pieces assumed at NT$1,500 new, resold as-is at 25 percent, NT$50 cleaning and NT$60 handling. Van capacity 6.0 loading metres (ldm), 0.25 per flat-packed and 0.9 per assembled piece; space used early in the round is charged at NT$150 per ldm, falling to zero as the van empties. A dedicated run is costed at 35 minutes and 14 km. Crew time is paid in full at the crew rate. On top, the crew bonus our proposal suggests, NT$50 for every extra piece loaded and at most three per stop (28 % of the NT$179 an extra piece adds), is included in every figure here and is a slider. Unknown grades on assisted bookings are estimated as grade B at NT$4,500. Credits include the slot, disassembly and neighbour bonuses. The planner uses cheapest insertion under the capacity limit. Phase one dispatches manually; this view is the phase-two tool for live tour-position pricing.
